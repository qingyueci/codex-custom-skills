# Secondary Text and Countertext

Use this reference whenever deciding whether secondary text is needed or writing it.

## Decide the relation first

Choose exactly one mode:

- **Preserve:** sincere happiness, lightness, tenderness, wonder, or calm. Use no secondary text by default.
- **Resonance:** emotional ambiguity without a defensible target. Add a brief companion line only if it contributes a new image or rhythm.
- **Tension:** philosophical, universal, or absolute claims. Add a question, limit, cost, exception, or consequence.
- **Satire:** self-deception, hypocrisy, convenient romance, institutional absurdity, or hidden cost. Add an external countertext.

Do not manufacture a target from a happy or light source. When evidence is mixed, step down one level: Satire → Tension → Resonance → Preserve.

## Generate and select

For Resonance, Tension, or Satire, write three internal candidates before choosing one. Do not show all three unless the user is in Explore mode.

Score candidates from 0-2 on:

- **Fit:** matches the selected relation mode and the source temperature;
- **Specificity:** adds a concrete image, boundary, cost, aftermath, logistics, or consequence;
- **Function:** resonates, complicates, or punctures without blurring those purposes;
- **Restraint:** stays literary rather than insulting, motivational, or meme-like;
- **Novelty:** avoids sentence structures already used in the current batch or recent conversation.

Select a candidate scoring at least 8/10. Rewrite if none qualify.

For Preserve, do not generate candidates merely to fill space. Let image-text, material, and composition carry the atmosphere.

## Useful Tension and Satire angles

- expiry, deadline, season, or warranty;
- receipt, evidence, witness, missing proof, or changed records;
- hidden cost, unpaid labor, inconvenience, or logistics;
- aftermath, residue, empty room, changed lock, or returned parcel;
- convenient excuse, selective memory, or romanticized self-deception;
- institutional language used sparingly against an emotional claim;
- controlled self-mockery when the speaker is also the target.

Prefer an imageable consequence over an abstract verdict.

## Anti-repetition

Inspect countertext already used in the current batch and recent conversation. Do not reuse the same rhetorical skeleton twice in succession.

After one use, avoid defaulting again to:

- `X只负责……不负责……`;
- `不是X，是你……`;
- `X无罪，你替它……`;
- `永远负责……季节负责……`;
- a neat reversal that merely completes or beautifies the source.

Change both the attack angle and sentence rhythm, not only the nouns.

## Hard failures

Reject countertext that:

- injects cynicism into sincere happiness, playfulness, tenderness, or calm;
- treats every philosophical line as something that must be mocked;
- makes the original sound wiser, sadder, or more romantic;
- repeats the original in different words;
- gives therapy advice or motivational guidance;
- relies on a generic internet punchline;
- insults the author, speaker, or reader;
- introduces a claim that the source does not support;
- becomes longer or more lyrical than the source quote.
