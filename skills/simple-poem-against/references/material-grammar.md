# Material and Composition Grammar

Use this reference when choosing imagery, image-text placement, paper behavior, color, or composition.

## Canvas families

Choose shape before placing text or imagery. Treat aspect ratio as part of the meaning, not an export setting.

### 3:5 vertical — fall, duration, solitude

- Use for long vertical movement, rain, roads, windows, standing figures, time, descent, or text-led negative space.
- Build one clear top-to-bottom path. Avoid leaving the lower third as unused filler.
- Prefer a narrow cluster, edge spill, threshold, or vertical reflection.

### 4:5 vertical — balance, warmth, general use

- Use for balanced image-text work, portraits, domestic scenes, short quotations, and most image-led posters.
- Let the eye move between two or three zones without forcing a tall narrative.
- Use as the fallback when the source gives no strong directional evidence.

### 1:1 square — object, pause, compression

- Use for one short line, one centered object, a specimen, seal-like mass, or conceptual stillness.
- Keep one dominant relation. Do not shrink a vertical poster into a square.
- Avoid long text, stacked metadata, or more than two competing zones.

### 4:3 horizontal — relation, dialogue, landscape

- Use for two people or objects, companionship, horizons, rooms, dialogue, journeys, domestic happiness, or left-to-right cause and response.
- Reserve side space for relation and distance, not decorative emptiness.
- Use split spread, horizon, opposing objects, or lateral sequence compositions.

When making image-led and type-led variants of the same source, keep the same canvas so the comparison is about hierarchy. Change canvas only when the user explicitly asks to compare shapes.

## Variant proportions

### Image-led

- Let imagery occupy roughly 35%-55% of the canvas.
- Keep roughly 40%-60% quiet or low-information field.
- Embed one or two image-text fragments into a real host surface.
- Make the image the first read at thumbnail size.

### Type-led

- Let typography occupy roughly 45%-70% of the canvas.
- Do not require a separate photograph.
- Let imagery appear as a force acting on paper or selected words.
- Transform only one or two meaningful words.

These are working ranges, not rigid geometry. Break them when the source or user explicitly calls for another density.

## Source-quote typography

Treat the main source quote as literature before treating it as a graphic shape.

- For classical verse, paired lines, or a requested ancient and restrained mood, default to the same optical size, weight, type family, character spacing, alignment, and level baseline across parallel lines.
- Let hierarchy come from placement, line spacing, negative space, ink density, or a restrained color field before changing font size.
- Let semantic motion such as ascent, victory, wind, freedom, or breakthrough act on surrounding paper, image fragments, shadows, or accents. Do not automatically turn that meaning into diagonal text, staircase baselines, rotated characters, or an oversized keyword.
- Distinguish main quote from image-text. A ghosted background character or material fragment may change scale only when the main quote remains intact and the extra layer is clearly subordinate.
- For an emphasized word, prefer color, underprint, abrasion, overprint, or a narrow material interruption while preserving its size and baseline.

Break these rules only when the user explicitly requests expressive, disruptive, poster-like typography or approves a reference that clearly uses it.

## Host surfaces for image-text

Place image-text on or inside a meaningful surface:

- flowing water, puddle, reflection, condensation, or fogged glass;
- wall, road, doorway, fabric, smoke, shadow, torn fiber, or printed photograph;
- a color field, horizon, fold, adhesive residue, or erased block.

Match perspective, curvature, direction, blur, and material behavior to the host. Do not float atmospheric words over unrelated empty space.

## Material actions

- **Rain or water:** darkened fibers, tide lines, downward runs, capillary bloom, wrinkles, droplets at lower stroke endings, reflected or submerged baselines.
- **Wind:** lifted corners, one-direction displacement, stretched spacing, drifting fragments, bent fibers.
- **Heat or summer:** faded pigment, sun bleaching, brittle paper, mirage-like registration, softened adhesive.
- **Cold or snow:** frost masking, whitened interruption, compressed spacing, brittle cracks.
- **Night or shadow:** projected doubles, silvered reflections, partial occlusion, delayed afterimages.
- **Time or decay:** oxidation, abrasion, ghost type, fold memory, adhesive stains, repeated overprinting.

Choose one primary action and at most one supporting action.

## Physical rules

- Water moves downward, gathers below, and spreads along fibers.
- Reflections sit opposite their sources and distort according to the surface.
- Droplets hang from plausible lower edges, not from every stroke.
- Wind displacement follows one dominant direction.
- Shadows agree with the implied light.
- Use torn collage or abstract print boundaries when literal continuity would create impossible physics.

## Diversity control

Do not default to warm beige paper, a lower-middle torn photo, and cobalt or vermilion accents.

When recent outputs are available, change at least three of these five dimensions:

1. composition family;
2. paper tone;
3. accent hue;
4. image or text cluster position;
5. rhetorical sentence structure.

Rotate among full-width strip, edge spill, split spread, threshold, reflection, diagonal force, type island, irregular memory, and single-relation compositions.

Allow ivory, cool gray, faded blue, muted pink, charcoal, oxidized tan, or other plausible printed stocks. Keep one main saturated accent occupying roughly 1%-4% of the canvas.

## Hard avoids

- full-frame cinematic photography that hides the paper;
- commercial movie-poster or advertising hierarchy;
- generic quotation cards;
- glossy mockups, neon, cyberpunk, anime, or fashion drama;
- dense scrapbook decoration and unrelated fragments;
- uniform weather effects pasted over every character;
- tiny-photo-plus-colored-dot repetition;
- theatrical 3D paper effects that overpower the printed zine language.
